# Hooks and beats

## Three hook shapes

1. **Face plus one line.** Frame 0: the maker says one sentence. Hard cut by 1.5 s to the product
   doing it. Needs 3–5 s of the maker on camera (a phone is enough).
2. **The magic trick.** The product does something surprising before second 1, with no setup
   on screen: one typed request, and the result appears.
3. **Pain to calm.** Second 0: the familiar mess (drawn as an unbranded stand-in, never a real
   competitor). By second 2 it collapses into the product. One pain shot only.

A short, plain first caption is part of the hook ("I fired my spreadsheet."), and it must still
meet the read time (a 5-word line needs at least 1.5 s fully on screen).

## A beat plan

| Part | Length | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 s | Stop the scroll: a face or the product moving |
| Reveal | 2–5 s | What it is, in one shown action |
| The trick | 10–15 s | The thing only this product does, end to end, on real UI |
| 2–4 proof beats | 8–12 s each | One claim each, shown, with its own caption |
| End card | 4–8 s | Name, one line, how to get it (a URL or one install command) |

Write the table before touching `film.yaml`: time, beat name, on screen, caption. Name beats
for what happens (`ask`, `drop`, `result`), not by number, because cutdowns and music cues refer
to them by name.

## Music on the plan

Mark where the music's drop lands (usually the reveal of the trick) and the final hit (the end
card). Those become beats; see [sound-and-loudness](../../sound-and-loudness/SKILL.md).

## Tone

Pick one and hold it: punchy and clean, restrained and elegant, deadpan, or trailer-scale. Humour
comes from the product's own absurdity, not from trying.
